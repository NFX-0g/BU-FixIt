BU FixIt: Product Requirements Document

Course: BU-COS 209

Team: Team Teal

Document: Product Requirements Document (PRD)

Version: 1.0


1. Product Overview

BU FixIt is a web app for students living in Babcock University halls. A student can report a fault in their room (a broken socket, a leaking tap, a bad door lock and so on) and then see what is happening with that report until it is fixed. Hall porters and hall admins get their own page where they can see all the reports for their hall, update the status, and keep a record of who the job was passed to.

Right now a student has to find the porter, explain what is wrong, and wait. After that there is no way to know if anyone is working on it or when someone will show up. BU FixIt gives every fault a record and a status that the student can check.


2. Problem Statement

Students in the halls do not have a clear way to report faults and follow them up. From our own experience and from the 18 students we spoke to:

- Reports are made in several different ways: to the hall porter, on WhatsApp, to the hall admin, to the block senator, or on a written form. There is no single way that everyone uses.
- Most students never get feedback. Only 4 of the 16 students who reported a fault said they got updates.
- Waiting time is unpredictable. Some faults were fixed the same day, while others took weeks, more than a month, or were never fixed.
- Because nothing is tracked, students keep going back to remind people. 9 of the 18 students followed up more than once.
- Some students end up spending their own money (3 of 18) or giving up and living with the fault (7 of 18).

One of us had a real example in a 100 level hostel. A hole in the toilet wall let a rat in. We complained several times and nothing happened for weeks. After about two weeks the school demolished the entire toilet and bathroom block without any warning, and it took a few more weeks to rebuild. A small fault grew into a big one because nobody was keeping track of it.

So the problem is not that nobody wants to fix things. It is that reports are verbal, nothing is properly recorded, and students cannot see what is going on.


3. Research and Interview Findings

3.1 How we did the research

We did two things. First, we used our own experience as students living in the halls to understand how maintenance reports normally work. Second, one of us went out of his comfort zone and interviewed 18 students face to face, mostly friends and people he knew around the school. He asked each person the same 13 questions and wrote down their answers. They came from 13 different halls and from 100 level to 400 level. The halls were Winslow, Nelson Mandela, Gideon Troopers, Welch, Bethel Splendor, Neal Wilson, Samuel Akande, Queen Esther, Crystal, Havilah Gold, Ameyo Adadevoh, Platinum and Nyberg.

We were not able to interview any porters, hall admins or maintenance staff. This is listed under the open questions in section 14.

3.2 Who has the problem

Students who live in the halls, which means anyone who has had something break in their room. Among the 16 students who reported a fault, the faults were plumbing (5), electrical (4), furniture (3), carpentry (2) and other (2).

3.3 How students cope today

How the 16 students reported their fault:

- Hall porter in person: 4
- WhatsApp: 4
- Hall admin: 3
- Block senator: 3
- Written form: 2

How long the fix took (the same 16 students):

- Same day: 2
- 1 to 3 days: 4
- 4 to 7 days: 3
- 1 to 4 weeks: 3
- More than a month: 2
- Never fixed: 2

Other results:

- Got updates on their report: 4 said yes, 5 said sometimes and 7 said no (out of 16)
- Followed up more than once: 9 of 18
- Paid for a repair themselves: 3 of 18
- Gave up and lived with the fault: 7 of 18
- Average satisfaction with how reports are handled: 2.7 out of 5
- Said phone status tracking would be useful (4 or 5 out of 5): all 18, and 13 of them gave 5

Respondents 3 and 11 said they had not reported a fault, but they still answered the questions about reporting. So we used only the 16 who said yes for the reporting questions (how they reported, how long it took, updates), and all 18 for the rest.

3.4 What we noticed

- Updates matter a lot. The 9 students who got no updates gave an average satisfaction of 1.9 out of 5. The 5 who sometimes got updates gave 3.0, and the 4 who got updates gave 4.25.
- Speed matters too. The 2 students whose faults were fixed the same day gave an average of 5 out of 5. The 3 who waited 1 to 4 weeks gave an average of 1.3.
- The students who paid also gave up. All 3 students who paid for a repair themselves (respondents 4, 6 and 9) also said they gave up and lived with the fault. Their waits were 1 to 4 weeks, more than a month, and never fixed.
- There is no single place to report. Five different ways were used, and a few students asked for one place where everyone could report and get feedback.
- In their comments, students asked for updates or knowing when someone is coming, faster repairs, a deadline for fixing faults, an easier way to report without looking for the porter, uploading pictures of the fault, and more attention on electrical faults because they can be dangerous.
- It is not bad for everyone. Respondents 5, 8 and 12 were happy with how fast their fault was handled, but even they asked for easier reporting.

3.5 Why the current ways are not enough

- A verbal report leaves no record, so it is easy for it to be forgotten. One student said it feels like reports just disappear.
- You have to be around and find the porter at the right time. One student said a digital system would help when you are not always near the hall office.
- WhatsApp messages are quick but they get buried, and nobody is clearly responsible for them.
- Nobody has to give a deadline or an update, so students have to keep chasing.
- Porters and admins cannot easily see every open fault in one place, so there is no clear way to tell a dangerous fault from a small one.

3.6 Why this matters

It is easy to think a broken socket or a small hole in the wall is not a big deal. But small faults left alone get worse, and that is what happened with the toilet and the rat. A fault that is actually dangerous, like a faulty electrical connection, should not sit in the queue behind a light bulb. Students also lose money when they fix things themselves, and they lose trust when nobody answers them. A simple way to record reports and see their status solves part of this without needing the school to change everything else.

3.7 Limits of our research

- 18 responses is a small number, and the people were friends and contacts, not a random group. The results do not speak for every student.
- All the interviews were done by one person, so the way the questions were asked may have affected some answers.
- The question about phone tracking was easy to say yes to, so 18 out of 18 should not be treated as proof that every student wants the app.
- Some groups are tiny (for example only 2 people had same-day fixes), so those averages are only a rough idea.
- We have not heard from porters, hall admins, senators or maintenance staff, so we only know the student side.


4. Business Objectives

BU FixIt is meant to:

1. Make it easier for a student to report a fault from anywhere, without having to find the porter.
2. Make sure every fault report is recorded and cannot just be forgotten.
3. Let students see where their report is, so they do not have to keep going back.
4. Give hall porters and admins one place to see everything that is open in their hall.
5. Reduce the number of faults that take weeks or never get fixed.
6. Reduce cases where students pay for repairs or live with dangerous faults.
7. Help the school see which kinds of faults happen most and how long they take to fix.


5. Target Users

- Students living in the halls. They create an account, report faults and check the status of their reports. Students log their problems themselves.
- Hall porters and hall admins (hall staff). They see the reports for their own hall, update the status and record who the job was passed to.

Maintenance staff (electricians, plumbers, carpenters) do not use the system directly in version 1. Hall staff pass the job on to them the way it is done now.


6. Stakeholders

- Students in the halls: they want easy reporting, a visible status and faster repairs. They use the system.
- Hall porters: they want an organised list of faults instead of verbal complaints. They use the system.
- Hall admins: they want to see all the faults in their hall and follow them up. They use the system.
- Block senators (students with higher authority in each hostel block): they help students get their complaints heard. They do not use the system in version 1.
- Maintenance staff (electrical, plumbing, carpentry): they need to know clearly what to fix and where. They do not use the system in version 1.
- School management and student affairs: they care about accountability, safety and the cost of materials. They do not use the system in version 1.
- Team Teal (the developers): we need a clear scope that we can build.

The titles above come from how we know the halls work. If any official title is different, it should be corrected (see the open questions).


7. Proposed Solution

A web app that works well on a phone browser, since students are often not at a desk or near the hall office. The flow is:

1. A student logs in and fills in a short form: hall, room number, type of fault (electrical, plumbing, carpentry, furniture or other) and a description. They can add a photo.
2. The report is saved with the status "Submitted".
3. The hall porter or admin sees it on their page and marks it "Acknowledged". Once they have passed it on to the right maintenance person, they mark it "Assigned".
4. The status then changes to "In progress" and later "Fixed". The student can see each change, and staff can add a short note like "waiting for materials".
5. If nothing happens, the student can send a reminder from the app instead of walking back to the porter.

Since the whole history of each report is saved, it is also easy to see how long repairs take and which faults get stuck.

How the solution answers what we found:

- Five different ways to report and no single place. BU FixIt gives every student one place to report.
- Only 4 of 16 got updates. Every report has a visible status and students are notified when it changes.
- Students keep following up (9 of 18). There is a reminder button, and the report is on record so nobody has to keep explaining it again.
- Reports feel like they disappear. Every report is saved with its full history.
- Dangerous faults get ignored. An urgent flag lets electrical and similar faults stand out.
- Students asked for photos. Reports can have a photo attached.
- Nobody has a deadline. There is a days-open counter and optional target fix times.


8. User Stories

- As a student, I want to create an account so that I can report faults from my own phone.
- As a student, I want to report a fault with my hall, room number and description so that the right people know what is wrong and where.
- As a student, I want to attach a photo so that the person fixing it understands the problem before they come.
- As a student, I want to see the current status of my report so that I do not have to keep asking the porter.
- As a student, I want to get a notification when my report changes so that I know when someone is coming.
- As a student, I want to send a reminder on an old report so that it does not get forgotten.
- As a hall porter or admin, I want to see all open reports for my hall so that I can keep track of what is pending.
- As a hall porter or admin, I want to update the status and add a note so that students are kept informed.
- As a hall porter or admin, I want to record which type of maintenance worker a report was passed to so that nothing gets lost between me and them.


9. Functional Requirements

- FR-01 (Must): A student can register and log in.
- FR-02 (Must): A student can submit a fault report with hall, room number, fault category and description.
- FR-03 (Must): A student can see a list of their own reports and the current status of each.
- FR-04 (Must): Every report has one of these statuses: Submitted, Acknowledged, Assigned, In progress, Fixed.
- FR-05 (Must): Hall staff can log in and see all reports for their own hall.
- FR-06 (Must): Hall staff can update the status of a report and add a short note.
- FR-07 (Must): Hall staff can record which type of maintenance worker (electrical, plumbing, carpentry, other) a report was passed to.
- FR-08 (Should): Hall staff can filter reports by status and category.
- FR-09 (Should): A student can attach a photo to a report.
- FR-10 (Should): A student gets a notification when the status of their report changes.
- FR-11 (Should): A student can send a reminder on a report that has not been updated.
- FR-12 (Could): A report can be flagged as urgent or dangerous (for example electrical faults).
- FR-13 (Could): The system shows how many days a report has been open.
- FR-14 (Could): A target fix time can be set for each fault category.


10. Non-Functional Requirements

These targets are our starting proposals and would need to be agreed with the school.

- Performance: at least 95% of pages and report submissions should respond within 3 seconds under normal use.
- Usability: a student should be able to submit a report in under 2 minutes, and the form must work properly on a phone screen.
- Security: passwords must never be stored in plain text. Students can only see their own reports. Hall staff can only see reports for their own hall.
- Privacy: room numbers and student details should only be visible to the student who made the report and to the staff of that hall.
- Reliability: pressing submit twice by accident must not create duplicate reports. A failed submission should not lose what the student typed in, where possible.
- History: every status change is saved with the date and time, so we can measure how long repairs take.
- Availability: the system should be available at all hours, since faults can happen any time. The exact uptime target is to be agreed.
- Scalability: it should handle all the students in the halls using it at the same time. The real numbers are to be confirmed.


11. Scope and Priorities (MVP)

We used MoSCoW to decide what goes into the first version.

Must have (the MVP):

- Registration and login for students and hall staff
- Submitting a fault report
- Students tracking the status of their own reports
- Hall staff page to view reports, update status and record assignment

Should have:

- Filtering reports by status and category
- Photo upload
- Notifications when a status changes
- Reminder button

Could have:

- Urgent or dangerous flag
- Days-open counter
- Target fix times for each fault category

Won't have in this version:

- Accounts for maintenance staff or block senators
- Online payments or collecting money for repairs
- Automatic prioritising or AI sorting of reports
- Tracking of materials or stock
- A separate mobile app (a mobile-friendly web app is enough for now)

The MVP is kept small on purpose. It cannot make repairs faster on its own, because that depends on people and materials. What it fixes is the part that is clearly broken: reports are not recorded, and students cannot see what is happening.


12. Acceptance Criteria

FR-02: Submit a fault report

- The student must be logged in.
- Hall, room number, fault category and description must be filled in.
- If something is missing, the system shows a clear error and does not save the report.
- When the report is saved, its status is "Submitted" and the student can see it in their list straight away.
- Pressing submit twice does not create two reports.

FR-03 and FR-04: Track status

- A student can only see their own reports.
- Each report shows its current status, the date it was submitted and the date of the last update.
- The status can only be one of the five allowed statuses.

FR-05 and FR-06: Hall staff handling reports

- Hall staff must be logged in with a staff account.
- They only see the reports for their own hall.
- When staff change a status, the new status and the time are saved and the student can see it.
- Staff can add a short note when changing a status, for example "waiting for materials".

FR-07: Assigning to a worker type

- Staff can choose electrical, plumbing, carpentry or other when assigning.
- The report shows who it was assigned to and when.

FR-10: Notifications (Should)

- The student is notified when the status of their report changes.
- A student only gets notifications for their own reports.

FR-11: Reminder (Should)

- A student can send a reminder only on their own report that is not yet fixed.
- The reminder shows up for the hall staff.
- The system limits how often a reminder can be sent, so staff are not spammed.


13. Success Metrics

We would know BU FixIt is working if, after it has been used for a while, we see:

- The number of registered students and the number of reports submitted each month
- The percentage of reports acknowledged within 24 hours
- The percentage of reports that get at least one status update
- The average and longest time from "Submitted" to "Fixed", by fault type
- The number of reports that stay open for more than 2 weeks
- The number of reminders sent per report (fewer is better)
- Student satisfaction when we ask the same questions again (our starting point is 2.7 out of 5)


14. Assumptions and Open Questions

Things we are not sure about (assumptions):

- We think the porter writes down the room number and complaint in some way, but we are not sure how, or whether it is the same in every hall.
- We think the porter or hall admin is the one who contacts the maintenance people, but this is from our own experience and has not been confirmed.
- We suspect repairs are sometimes held back until several faults pile up, but this is only a guess.
- We assume students can log in with their matric number, but this has not been checked with the school.
- We assume porters and hall admins would be willing to update statuses regularly. If they do not, the system will not work well.
- We assume block senators mainly pass complaints along and do not need their own accounts in version 1.

Open questions:

1. How do porters and hall admins currently record complaints, and is it the same in every hall?
2. How do they pass jobs on to maintenance staff, and how long does that step take?
3. What causes the longest delays, and who decides when materials are bought?
4. What are the correct official titles for the people involved (porter, hall admin, maintenance unit)?
5. Would the school allow students to log in using their matric numbers?
6. How exactly do block senators fit in? Should they be given a role in a later version?
7. Does the school want fix-time targets, and who would set them?
8. In a later version, should maintenance staff get their own accounts so they can see their jobs?
